# Benatiger

## What it is

Benatiger is a single-player deduction game set at a lost-property bureau during four night shifts. It is for players who enjoy examining objects, comparing testimony with physical evidence, and deciding which of two claimants should take each item home.

## Launch and onboarding

1. A fresh launch opens directly at the reception counter. The masthead reads “Benatiger”, the night heading reads “RAIN”, and the case title is “The Music Box”. The counter shows the music box, a lamp, and a brass bell. The prompt reads “Lost Property. Night service. Ring once and wait by the lamp.”
2. Tap “Ring the brass bell” or the bell itself. The two visitors appear as “Eleanor Vale” and “Arthur Bell”. The prompt becomes “Rain drips from a coat hem. A woman and a man with a music folder hum the same four notes.”
3. Tap either visitor to hear their claim, or tap the music box or “Bring the item to the bench” to start examining it. Ringing the bell also reveals “Reception”, “Workbench”, and “Receipt” along the bottom.
4. There is no separate tutorial, setup form, or onboarding permission prompt. When a case has already been started, a cold launch returns to the saved case and its last main screen.

The labels below are quoted in English. US and UK variants are called out where a control or card title differs. On first launch in Arabic, “علبة الموسيقى” opens with “مكتب المفقودات. خدمة ليلية. اقرع الجرس مرة وانتظر عند المصباح.” and “اقرع الجرس النحاسي”. On first launch in Traditional Chinese, “音樂盒” opens with “失物招領，夜間值勤。按一聲鈴，在燈下等候。” and “按響黃銅鈴”.

## Screens

### “Reception”

The night and case title sit above an illustrated counter. In each new case, “Ring the brass bell” and the bell hotspot reveal the two claimants, their names, and the case’s arrival text. Each named portrait opens that visitor’s conversation. After the bell, the item hotspot and “Bring the item to the bench” both open “Workbench”.

The masthead is available before and after ringing. Its speaker icon is labeled “Turn sound off” when sound is on and “Turn sound on” when sound is off. Turning sound off also stops any clip currently playing. The tray icon, “Night board & archive”, opens the case list. Its replacement, the X icon labeled “Close archive”, returns to the previous screen. These controls are available throughout the game. There is no separate settings screen.

The bottom navigation appears after ringing. “Reception” returns to the counter, “Workbench” opens the object examination, and “Receipt” opens the release form. Once a case is released, “Letter” takes the place of “Receipt”.

In “The Music Box”, “Play the four notes” plays the tune. The printed line is “G · B · D · B   (the last note comes after a pause)”. Asking “Eleanor Vale” to “Show me how you wind it.” displays her demonstration of the hinge.

Additional controls appear at specific inspection steps.

- “The Music Box” offers “Keep a thumb on the left pin” before lifting the lid. The waveform icon labeled “Play the four notes” plays the tune. Pulling out the paper reveals “Repair slip” and “The same signature is on the claim form.”
- “The Ticket Punch” offers “Slide a blank ticket between the jaws” before squeezing. Under “Choose the cut that matches the route stub.”, “Square cut ■”, “Split cut ⌄”, and “Round cut ●” select the cut to compare.
- “The Rehearsal Metronome” has a waveform icon labeled “Run the metronome”. It plays the beats and animates a five-dot strip. The printed rhythm is “1 · 2 · 3 · [pause] · 4 · 5”. Under “Which beat arrives after the long gap?”, “Second beat”, “Third beat”, and “Fourth beat” select a beat to compare.
- “The Signal Lantern” shows “Click the shutter into one of its three detents.” “Amber · first stop”, “Green · middle stop”, and “Blue · third stop” change the shutter position.
- “The Folding Camera” shows “Align the two scratches”. The left arrow, center icon, and right arrow are labeled “Shift frame left”, “Center frame in the gate” in US English or “Centre frame in the gate” in UK English, and “Shift frame right”. They move the test frame. The film can also be dragged directly.
- “The Warded Keys” shows “Fit two teeth to the warded lock. Compare depth before checking the fit.” “Short square”, “Long square”, “Deep step”, and “Shallow step” toggle the key profiles for the fit test.
- “The Untagged Suitcase” lets the player release each clasp, pull out the tray, and draw out the paper. The extracted paper shows “Delivery references”.

The book icon labeled “Observations” opens the notebook. It contains the observations collected for the current case, the printed tune in “The Music Box”, and any filed objection. “Close the notebook” is the X button. After a disputed release, “Keep objection in the notebook” files the displayed “The claimant objects” note. “Read the earlier objection” expands or collapses it in the notebook. On a failed camera or key comparison, the info icon labeled “Why the pieces do not match” opens “Still at the bench” with a fuller hint. “Close the detail” dismisses it.

### “Receipt”

“Release to” shows both claimants as name buttons. Tap one to select the recipient. “Evidence cards” shows the attachment slots. In “The Untagged Suitcase”, the heading is “Copies from the archive”. Tap “Insert a card”, an occupied slot named for its card, or “Read the available cards” to open the card sheet.

The sheet is headed “Attach or remove a card”. Each card shows its title and details. “Attach” fills a slot and “Remove” frees it. The accessibility labels include the card title, as in “Attach · Signed paper shim”. “Back to the receipt” closes the sheet. The four cases that allow testimony as evidence also show “How to link the evidence”, which expands a case-specific hint.

Ordinary cases start with a shared clue card. The titles are “The shared tune”, “The dented handle”, “Cinnamon and shift sticker”, “Polished handles”, “Red wool with gray flecks” in US English or “Red wool with grey flecks” in UK English, “Green optician's felt”, “Marked opening tempo”, “Amber cue paint”, “Red dress and open wave”, “A streak on the print”, and “Locksmith's stamped ring”. Findings add cards with the observation titles listed under “Workbench”. In the testimony cases, asking a question adds a card titled “Statement · ” followed by the visitor’s name. The final suitcase uses filed copies titled for the earlier cases.

“Receipt preview” shows “Choose a claimant” until a name is selected, then shows the name and attached card titles. When the form is ready, “Press the brass stamp” submits it. The player can also drag the illustrated stamp down, as indicated by “Pull the brass stamp down onto the receipt.” A successful release opens “Letter”. A disputed release returns to “Workbench”.

### “Letter”

“Release recorded” shows the recipient’s name with a seal. The screen displays the attached evidence and a case-specific story letter under “Letter”. “Open the intake drawer” opens “Night board & archive”. “Revisit the conversations” returns to “Reception” to select a visitor. Released cases retain “Reception” and “Workbench” for review.

After the twelfth release the letter and archive also show “The bureau is closed” and “All twelve things have gone home. The lamp goes dark, and the brass tiger on the door catches the first light.”

## Features

- Twelve lost-property deductions across “Rain”, “Last Tram”, “Dress Rehearsal”, and “Before Dawn”, with two claimants per case.
- “Reception” conversations with competing claims, a choice of questions, and post-release rereading.
- “Workbench” inspection with two sides, interactive parts, mechanical comparisons, and an “Observations” notebook.
- “Play the four notes” and “Run the metronome”, with printed alternatives and a visual metronome strip.
- “Evidence cards”, selectable recipients, a live “Receipt preview”, and an interactive brass stamp.
- Alternate physical-evidence and testimony combinations in four cases, with “How to link the evidence” hints.
- Case-specific objections, retry clues, filed releases, and story letters.
- “Night board & archive” for unlocking cases, resuming open cases, reviewing releases, and cross-referencing earlier cases in the finale.
- Persistent sound control, accessibility labels for icon controls, button alternatives for object gestures, support for larger system text and reduced motion, and haptic feedback on supported hardware.

## Behaviours that can look like bugs

- **The counter appears inactive on arrival.** “Reception”, “Workbench”, and “Receipt” appear after “Ring the brass bell”. Ring once for each new case.
- **The next inspection action disappears after the first clue.** “Turn the item to find the next point of interest.” means the remaining inspections are on the back. Tap “Turn over”. “Observations” says “Inspect a detail to add it to the notebook.” until the first inspection. The case’s evidence list grows as observations and eligible statements are collected.
- **A visitor’s second question disappears.** “One question for this visitor” is a case rule. After asking, the answer remains available. Switch to the other visitor or use physical evidence. After release, “Revisit the conversations” allows both responses to be read.
- **An inspection refuses the current setup.** “Still at the bench” gives a retry clue. Use “Keep a thumb on the left pin” before lifting the music-box lid. For the punch, use “Slide a blank ticket between the jaws” before squeezing and select “Split cut ⌄” before comparing. Use “Fourth beat” for the metronome and “Blue · third stop” for the lantern. For the camera, center the frame until the scratches overlap. For the keys, use “Short square” and “Deep step”. A third tooth cannot be added until one of the current two is deselected. “Two scratches are still visible.” and “The teeth do not seat.” offer “Why the pieces do not match” for more detail.
- **A part slips back during a gesture.** “The catch slipped back. Grip the marked part again.” asks for a fresh grip and a longer pull. “The paper slipped back. Pull it a little farther.” asks for more travel. “Still two marks. Adjust the film and release where they meet.” asks for alignment before letting go. “One clasp released. The other is still shut.” asks the player to tap the other clasp. The labeled inspection actions also advance these steps when their prerequisites are met. A comparison setting clears after its observation is recorded.
- **“Attach” or “Press the brass stamp” is disabled.** If the card slots are full, tap “Remove” on an attached card first. Select a recipient under “Release to”, then fill the displayed evidence slots. “The Green Thermos”, “The Ticket Punch”, “The Rehearsal Metronome”, and “The Signal Lantern” allow a one-card submission to receive feedback, but a successful release requires a valid pair. The stamp is briefly disabled while it presses.
- **Stamping sends the player back to the bench.** “The claimant objects” gives a case-specific reason. It can be filed with “Keep objection in the notebook”. The recipient and cards stay selected. Inspect more details or revisit the claims, then open “Receipt” and change the recipient or cards before stamping again. For the first case, a direct route is to collect “Signed paper shim”, select “Eleanor Vale”, attach the shim, and tap “Press the brass stamp”.
- **A later case row is locked.** “Awaiting prior releases” means all cases from earlier nights must be released first. Within each night, release its first case to open the other two. “The Untagged Suitcase” additionally requires both “The Folding Camera” and “The Warded Keys” to be released. “Look under the tray before releasing the case.” means the final inspection must be completed even if both archive cards are already attached. The correct final release is to “Elsie Holt” with “The Green Thermos” and “The Red Marionette” filed copies.
- **A successful release stays on the letter.** Tap “Open the intake drawer” and select an available case to continue. After “The bureau is closed”, the filed cases remain available to review.

## Starter content and resume

A fresh game starts with “The Music Box” available and the other eleven cases visible on the board. All cases, claimants, questions, clues, and letters are built-in story content. The initial archive contains no issued releases.

Progress saves automatically on the device, including the active case, its last main screen, bell status, observations, selected questions, receipt draft, and filed releases. Open cases can be resumed through “Open case · resume”. The archive and temporary detail sheets reopen through their controls. Sound preference also persists. There is no in-app reset control.

## Permissions

None.

## Absent

Login or accounts, in-app purchase, ads, analytics, user-generated content, account deletion flow, and the App Tracking Transparency prompt are all absent.

## Data and support

The game works offline. Case progress, filed releases, and the sound preference are kept on the device. There is no in-app “Contact” or “Support” control.

## Platform

- Native iPhone and iPad support. Minimum iOS and iPadOS version is 17.0.
- iPhone is portrait only. iPad supports portrait, upside-down portrait, and both landscape orientations. Wider layouts place some panels side by side.
- The app uses the first preferred language. It provides US English for the US English preference, Arabic with right-to-left layout, and Traditional Chinese for Traditional Chinese or the Taiwan, Hong Kong, and Macao Chinese preferences. UK English is the fallback. The preference affects the language and some wording. The case order and deduction rules are shared across editions.
- The game uses a dark appearance.
